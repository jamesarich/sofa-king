# Using Sofa King

This page covers the guide, the library, recordings, the channels the guide lists, multiview, the remote and keyboard controls, captions and the settings. [Getting started](getting-started.md) connects the app to a server first.

## Guide

The app opens where you left it. Left from Live, it opens full screen on the channel you last watched; Back shows the guide focused on that channel. Left from Library or DVR, it opens that tab, with nothing playing. When you have not watched a channel yet, or that channel is no longer in the lineup, it opens the guide. Settings → Live TV → Open on start can open the guide, the library or one chosen channel instead. With no server set, the guide shows Connect to your Sofa King server with the header's Settings button focused; press OK and [connect to the server](getting-started.md#3-connect-to-the-server).

### Moving around the guide

The Now chip, the Guide key and the Menu key return to the current time on the focused channel. Fast Forward and Rewind move the grid a whole day ahead and back, up to six days, keeping the time of day and the focused channel; the range above the channel names reads Tomorrow or the weekday, and a day's programs load the first time you move to it. Coming back to the app after half an hour or more away (Home, the screen off or standby) moves the grid to the current time on the focused channel, unless you had moved it to another day.

In the guide, Back moves from the grid to the selected filter chip, and from the chips to the open tab in the header; from there Back exits the app. Open search closes first.

### Search and filters

Search filters by channel name, number or current program, and lists a channel's later programs that match on a Later today line under its row, each with its start time; OK on one moves the grid to that program. The Search key starts voice search through the TV's speech recognizer and focuses the first match; the Search chip, a TV without a recognizer, or a dismissed recognizer open the text field instead. All, Favorites, Recent (the last ten channels watched full screen for at least 10 seconds, newest first) and category chips select channel groups, and the app reopens on the chip you chose last. Movies now, Sports now, Kids now and News now list the channels whose current program the guide files under that kind; each shows only while some channel has one, and the lists are read again every five minutes. On a server that offers it, the last chip, On later, opens a page of what is coming up today and tomorrow on the channels the guide shows, in Movies, Sports, Kids and Premieres (a new first episode of a season), soonest first, each with its day, time, channel and marks; OK on one opens its details, where Record, Record series, Add to watchlist and Watch work as in the guide, and Back returns to the guide.

### Preview

The focused channel plays in a muted preview after a 350 ms delay. After focus has rested on it for 5 seconds the preview's sound comes on, and after 10 seconds the channel opens full screen, continuing the preview's stream. On Android the muted preview leaves other apps' sound playing and takes the sound over only when its own comes on, and a channel that opened by itself lets the TV's screen saver start until you press a key. Settings → Live TV sets that delay (Off, 10 or 30 seconds) and turns preview sound off. The channel never opens by itself while TalkBack touch exploration on Android or VoiceOver on iOS or Apple TV is on, or while the preview shows Preview unavailable. Any key press restarts both timers and mutes the preview again. A touch or mouse press or scroll restarts them too, but after one the channel does not open by itself until the next key press, so scrolling the guide by hand never opens a channel under your finger. OK opens that channel full-screen at once. Back returns to the guide focused on the channel you were watching, including one you changed to in the player, with the preview muted. A filter or search that hides that channel switches back to All, and a time cursor left in the past moves to now. The channel just left does not reopen by itself until focus moves to another channel.

### Program details

The Info key, or holding OK on a program, opens its details: the image, title, episode, time with how long is left or until it starts, rating and description, with Watch, Add to favorites (or Remove from favorites) and Close, and Watch from start while the program is being recorded, which plays the recording from its start; on a TV the details open in a panel beside the grid, elsewhere in a sheet.

On a server that records, a program that has not ended also has Record, which schedules it (Cancel recording takes it off again), and Record series, which records every airing of its title on that channel (Stop series deletes the rule and the recordings it scheduled, keeping the ones already made); beside Record series, Padding steps a new rule's padding and the channel button switches it between this channel only and any channel. A sports program whose title or episode title names two sides, such as `Bears at Packers`, also has Record for each team, which records every sports program naming that team on any channel (Stop recording takes the team's rule off again); Record next 30 min, Record next hour and Record next 2 hours record the channel from now for that long, program or not, so a channel with no guide can be recorded too; the one-off Record keeps the server's padding. The server's [recordings](../../backend/docs/recordings.md) page describes what happens next. When the server does not take the change, a line under the buttons says so.

Add to watchlist puts the program's title on the household's [watchlist](../../backend/docs/api.md#watchlist), for every channel (Remove from watchlist takes it off again). When the guide lists the same title at other times or on other channels, More airings (with how many) lists them, soonest first, each with its day, time and channel and the New and Live marks; OK on one on now watches it, OK on a later one opens its details, and Back returns to the details you came from. Airings match by title, so a title that several shows share, such as a newscast, lists them all.

### Channel rows and refresh

Each channel shows its logo beside its number. The details above the grid show the focused program's episode label and title, a New badge for a first airing, its rating and its image, when the guide source has them. Times follow the device's 12- or 24-hour clock setting. A channel without programs says why: No listing when its guide source lists nothing then, No guide for this channel when the server has no guide source mapped to it, and Guide out of date when the guide source's data has run out.

The app shows its saved lineup while refreshing. Channels load before guide data. Automatic refresh runs every three hours and when the app returns to the front; Settings → Server → Reload requests a new load.

## Library

### Library tab

When the server keeps media libraries, the app shows a Library tab beside Live. On a TV both tabs sit in the header next to the app's name, Up from the guide's chips reaches them, and Settings stays the header's last button; on a phone the tabs are a bar along the bottom, and on a tablet, desktop or browser a rail at the side, with Settings as the last item; the bar or rail stays on every page but the full-screen players. On a TV the Guide key (or Menu) on any Library or DVR page opens the guide at Now. The Library tab reads its shelves again each time it is opened. A server without libraries shows no Library tab; the app asks again every five minutes, so the tab appears within five minutes of the first library being added.

The Library tab opens with a Search chip and a chip for each library at the top, then Continue watching (what you stopped partway through and the next episode of a show you are watching), the household's Watchlist, newest first, and each library's recent additions. Holding OK on a Continue watching card (a long touch or a right click elsewhere) offers Remove from Continue watching, which takes it off the shelf for the whole household until someone plays it again.

Search finds movies, shows, episodes and videos by title, show or cast across every library as you type, in rows of Movies, Shows, With and the people found (what the actors and directors whose names hold the search are in, named by the server, or by the search itself on an older server), Episodes and Videos; the remote's Search key on the Library tab starts it by voice, and Back returns to the Library tab. A card without a poster shows a frame from the video, or the library mark when there is none.

### Library grids

A library opens as a grid of posters with chips to sort by title, recently added, year, rating or recently watched and to show only what is unwatched, and a row of the library's genres to show only one; more load as you scroll. In title order, a library of 80 or more titles also has a row of first letters (# for titles that start with a digit or a symbol); OK on a letter moves to the first title under it, loading the grid that far. Cards show a bar for how far in you are, a check mark once watched (a rewatch stopped part way shows both and resumes) and, on a show, how many episodes are unwatched. OK opens an item's page; the remote's Play key plays a movie or episode straight from its card, and holding OK on a card (a long touch or a right click elsewhere) opens a menu, as the key comes up, with Play from beginning for something partway in and Mark watched or Mark unwatched. An item page's overview that runs past four lines takes focus, and OK shows the rest. With Settings → Library and DVR → Hide spoilers on, episodes you have not started keep their summaries back and blur their pictures.

On a phone, tablet, desktop or browser window 840 dp or wider, a library's grid stays on the left and the title you pick opens beside it, with Pick a title to see it here until you do. Picking another title from the grid takes the place of the one open, and Back closes the title page and leaves the grid. A title opened from the Library tab, search or Continue watching fills the window, as everywhere on a TV.

### Back and Watch Next

Back goes from an item's page to where you opened it and from a library's grid to the Library tab; on the Library tab it moves focus up to the tab in the header, and from there opens Live with focus on its tab, where one more Back exits. On Android TV, the home screen's Watch Next row (Continue watching on Google TV) carries the same list: selecting an entry plays it in the app from where you stopped, and Back returns to the Library tab. An entry removed from the row stays off it until it leaves Continue watching. Google TV reads the row through its Engage service once Google enrolls the app, kept on the TV and not synced to a Google account; until then the app writes the Watch Next row itself, which Google TV shows until it drops Watch Next in the second half of 2027.

### Item pages

An item's page shows its backdrop over a page tinted with the picture's dominant color (kept dark enough that text reads as it does elsewhere, fading in once found and shown at once with animations off), title, year, length, rating, genres and badges for the picture (4K, HD, HDR), a line with the sound of its first audio track, its audio and subtitle languages and the studio or network, the overview, who directed it, and its cast as a row of chips; OK on a cast member searches the libraries for that name, and Back returns to the page. Focus starts on Play, or Resume from the time you stopped, with Play from beginning beside it and Mark watched or Mark unwatched after; a movie's, show's or video's page then has Add to watchlist (Remove from watchlist once it is there), which puts it on the Library tab's Watchlist for the whole household; an episode's or a season's page ends the row with Go to show, which opens the show's page.

A show's page plays or resumes its next episode, lists its seasons in a row of chips (opening on the season with that episode, which takes focus when you come down to the row) and the season's episodes under them, each with its own still; OK on an episode plays it, resuming where you stopped, and holding OK on one (a long touch or a right click elsewhere) opens a menu with Details, which opens the episode's own page, Play from beginning when it is partway in, and Mark watched or Mark unwatched; Back from that page returns to the episode.

Under them, a movie's or show's page has Extras, its trailers, featurettes and other extras the server found beside its files, which OK plays, and back from one focus returns to it; and More like this, others in the library that share its genres, which OK opens.

### Music

A Music library opens as a grid of its artists' square pictures, with chips to list its albums instead, each on its cover with its artist and year, or its songs, each on its album's cover with its artist, and OK plays one; each view sorts by name, by date added, by when it was last played or by how often, albums also by year, and has a row of its genres; a Favorites chip lists the songs the household liked. A song counts as played once it has played for 30 seconds, which feeds Recently played, Most played and the radio. An artist's page shows the year they were born or formed and their country, a band's members, each of which searches the libraries, and their albums, newest first, and focus starts on the first; under the albums, Popular lists up to 10 of their songs that ListenBrainz's listeners play most, which play on through the list, and More like this the artists in the library that listeners play alongside them, then those sharing their genres; an artist's or album's Start radio plays on from it: the artist's own songs, those of artists listeners play alongside them and those sharing their genres, the songs listeners play most more often, never more than two of one artist in a row; an album's page names its artist, year, number of songs and length, with Play, which plays it from its first song, Shuffle, which plays its songs in a random order, and Go to artist, then its songs in disc and track order, each with its number (with its disc's, as in 2-05, on an album of several discs), its own artist when that differs from the album's, as on a compilation, and its length. OK on a song plays it and opens Now playing, which sits on a soft blurred copy of the album's cover tinted with its dominant color, and shows the album's cover with the song's title, artist and album, a seek bar, and Shuffle, Previous, Play or Pause, Next, Repeat and Favorite (a heart that likes the song for the household); the songs go on through the rest of the album, and Shuffle plays the album in a random order. When the server has the song's lyrics, a Lyrics button after Favorite shows them in the cover's place: synced lyrics light the line being sung and keep it near the top, and plain lyrics show as written. Where the app can hear what it plays (Android, Android TV, the desktop app, the web app and iOS so far; on iOS not for a song the server converts to HLS), a Visualizer button after Lyrics puts a MilkDrop-style picture behind Now playing, moving to the music; a different look takes over with each song and every 45 seconds, a few made for Sofa King first and then over a thousand classic MilkDrop presets in a shuffled order, and a Next look button beside it skips to another. Previous starts the song again once it is a few seconds in, and goes back a song otherwise. Repeat plays the album again after its last song, then the one song over and over, then turns off. Back leaves Now playing and the music plays on: away from a TV a bar under the pages shows the song with Play or Pause and Next, and a click on it opens Now playing again; on a TV a button with the song's title, beside Settings in the Library and DVR headers, opens it again, and Google TV's Now playing card and the remote's media keys control the music from anywhere. The music ends after the album's last song, or when a channel, a movie or a recording plays; Settings pauses it, and Play picks it up where it was. The music plays on when the app goes into the background or the screen locks, with its controls in the notification and on the lock screen on Android and iOS; a song paused there picks up where it was. The guide's preview is a channel, so on a TV opening Live ends the music. On a TV, Now playing dims and moves a little after half a minute without a key, so its cover does not mark the screen; the next key brings it back and does nothing else. A song found in search plays alone. The Library tab's Recently added row for a Music library shows its newest albums, and search finds artists, albums and songs, in rows after the others. Music has no watched state, resume or watchlist, so its pages and cards have no Mark watched, Play from beginning or Add to watchlist. On Apple TV, a Music library's grid, an artist's albums and an album's songs work the same way, without search or Go to artist; Now playing has no seek bar, only how far the song is, and Menu leaves it with the music playing on, including behind the app. Now playing at the top of the Library tab opens it again, and Control Center shows the song with Play or Pause, Next and Previous. tvOS's screen saver covers Now playing when the remote rests.

### Library player

Playing a library item opens the full-screen player. Left and Rewind step back 10 seconds and Right and Fast Forward 30, or the steps set in Settings → Library and DVR → Step back and Step forward (5 to 90 seconds each), which the bar's step buttons, the media keys and a double tap follow too; holding the key moves a minute and then five at a time. With the bar up the target shows on it under the title; with the bar down it stays down and a badge low in the middle names how far the presses move and where they land. Once the server has made a file's scrub thumbnails, a picture of the target shows above the bar's mark or above the badge, and above a dragged seek bar too. The target plays once you stop pressing, or at once on OK; Back while moving cancels it, and Back otherwise returns to the item's page with the episode you watched focused.

OK pauses and resumes and shows the bar; Play/Pause, Play and Pause (Space and K on a keyboard) do it without bringing the bar up, and a disc in the middle of the picture confirms it, with Paused at the top left while it holds. Up or Info show the bar, which shows where you are, when the item ends at the current time if it plays on, and its length.

Down shows the bar too, and on the bar moves into its buttons: Skip break while a recording plays a commercial break, Skip intro while an episode plays its intro, Skip credits while it plays its end credits, Audio and subtitles when the file has more than one audio track or has subtitles, Captions on a recording, Zoom, Sleep timer (see below), Speed, Start over once you are 30 seconds in, Next episode when the episode has one, and Back to live on a recording still being made, which leaves it for its channel, live. Left and Right move along them, OK presses one, and Up or Back returns to the picture. CC (C on a keyboard) turns subtitles off, or back on with the ones shown last; on a recording it switches its captions, as on live TV. The remote's Audio track key opens Audio and subtitles, Stop returns to the page, and a gamepad's shoulder buttons step back and forward as Left and Right do.

### Skip breaks, intros and credits

When a recording runs into a commercial break with Skip commercials set to With a button, the bar comes up with Skip break focused, so OK skips it. When an episode reaches the intro the server found in it (the theme and titles its show repeats in every episode), the bar comes up with Skip intro focused, so OK jumps past it; it is offered once per intro, once the episode plays, and not while Audio and subtitles is open. Nothing is skipped without a press unless Settings → Library and DVR → Skip intros is set to Automatically: then Skip intro counts down from 5 and skips the intro, and any key, a tap or a pause stops the countdown and leaves the button. The end credits the server found come up the same way, with Skip credits focused; when they run to the end of an episode that has a next one, the button reads Next episode and plays it at once. With Settings → Library and DVR → Skip credits set to Automatically, Skip credits counts down the same way and skips to the end, where Up next takes over. Set to Off, Skip commercials, Skip intros or Skip credits offers no button at all for that kind and lets it play.

### Mouse, touch and keyboard

With a mouse or touch, the bar has a Back button at the top left, the step-back, play and step-forward buttons and the same buttons in one row, and a seek bar you drag or tap; a double tap on the left or right third of a phone or tablet's picture steps back or forward as those settings say. On a desktop or in a browser, Space or K pauses and resumes, and moving the mouse shows the bar. The bar leaves 5 seconds after the last key, tap or mouse movement unless the item is paused.

### Speed, audio and subtitles

Speed steps through 1×, 1.25×, 1.5× and 2× with each press, keeping the voices' pitch; the bar's end time follows it, and each item starts at 1×.

In Audio and subtitles, picking another audio track plays the item again from where it is, and subtitles are drawn over the picture. Settings → Captions and subtitles sets the subtitles' size, how high they sit and whether a box is behind them. With a text subtitle on, Subtitle timing at the bottom of the card has Earlier and Later, which move the subtitles a quarter of a second a press, up to 10 seconds either way, for a file whose subtitles run ahead of the speech or behind it; each item starts with the file's own timing. A forced subtitle track, which carries only the dialogue the audio does not, is on from the start; the others start off. The tracks you pick in a show are kept on the device, by language, and its other episodes start with them. Subtitles inside the file and subtitle files beside it, such as the ones Bazarr downloads, are listed, the files marked External; when a show starts its next episode with your subtitle language, a file in that language wins over the subtitles inside the video. Picture subtitles (PGS from Blu-ray, VobSub from DVD) are drawn into the picture by the server, so picking one, or turning one off, plays the item again from where it is.

### Conversion, progress and Up next

The server plays a file your device can play as it is; for anything else it converts the file as it plays, and a jump past what it has converted restarts it there, which takes a moment. When the server is already converting as many files as it allows, the player shows Waiting for a free converter and asks again every 5 seconds; after 20 seconds it shows the failure card.

Where you stop is saved every 10 seconds, when you leave and when another app comes to the front; the server marks an item watched past 90%. Returning to the app picks up where you were.

When an episode ends, an Up next card shows the next one, the next in its season or the first of the next season (Specials aside), and plays it after 10 seconds; OK or Play plays it at once and Back returns to the page. After two episodes in a row that Up next played by itself, with no key, tap or click since, the card asks Still watching? instead: OK or Keep watching plays the next one, and with no answer it returns to the page after 30 seconds. Anything else, or a show's last episode, returns to the page at the end.

A stream that stops more than 10 seconds before the item's end dropped rather than finished, so it plays again from where it stopped, up to twice until playback moves on by 30 seconds, and then shows the failure card instead of Up next.

## DVR

On a server that records, the app shows a DVR tab beside Live and Library. It lists what is recording now, then what was recorded, newest first, then what is scheduled, soonest first, with the recordings folder's free space at the top. Each row shows the title, the episode, the channel and when it aired, and whether it was watched, failed or is still recording. A scheduled recording the server flags as in conflict, because more recordings overlap it than the server has playback slots, shows Won't record: other recordings fill the server's slots then in red, and the program's details in the guide say the same under Cancel recording. A recording that is protected says so. Play opens a recording in the full-screen player; one started before shows Resume, which picks up where the household stopped, and From start. A recording still being made plays as it grows. Commercial breaks the server found show on the seek bar, and Settings → Library and DVR → Skip commercials set to Automatically makes playback jump over them. The first recording takes focus when the tab opens. Protect on a finished recording has cleanup skip it when the recordings disk runs short, and Unprotect lets cleanup take it again; the row shows Protected while it is kept. Stop, Cancel or Delete ends a running recording, takes a scheduled one off or deletes a finished one and its file, after a second press confirms it; the button then names the recording, and Keep it beside it backs out. Stopping within the last tenth of a recording marks it watched. Below the recordings, Series lists the series rules: Record repeats too (or New episodes only) changes which airings a rule takes, Keep steps how many recordings it keeps through All, 1, 3, 5 and 10, and Stop series deletes the rule and the recordings it scheduled after a second press (Keep it backs out), keeping the ones already made. Padding steps through Default (1 minute before, 2 after), None, 5 min, 15 min and Long games (30 minutes past the end), and Any channel, shown on a rule limited to one channel, makes it take every channel. A rule's priority, and putting a rule back on one channel, are set on the server's portal. With the guide's search open on a server that records, Record everything matching “text” beside the field (for a search of two or more characters) adds a keyword rule: it records, on any channel, every program whose title, episode title or description holds that word or phrase, and Series lists it by its text. Stop recording “text” takes the rule off, deleting the recordings it scheduled. Back goes from a recording to the DVR tab, from the tab's list up to the tab in the header, and from there to Live with focus on its tab, where one more Back exits.

## Channels

Channels come from the server's lineup. The app lists a channel when the server reports it as playable and gives it a numeric display number, and lists them in number order. The server tags each channel with its lineup group, such as News, and each tag is a guide category with its own chip; a channel with several tags is under each, and one without a tag is under Other. Chips show how many channels they list.

## Multiview

On a server that [draws multiview](../../backend/docs/api.md#multiview), the live player's bar has a Multiview button, before Guide. It opens a list of channels beside the picture, with the channel you were watching ticked, then your favorites, your recent channels and the rest; tick two to four and press Watch. The server draws them into one picture: two side by side, or three or four in a grid. Only one channel's sound plays, marked by a white border and a speaker by its name. The arrows move the sound to the next tile that way, and OK watches the channel with the sound full screen; with a mouse or touch, a tap on a tile moves the sound there and a double tap watches it. Names show for 5 seconds after each press. Back returns to the list to change the channels, and Back from the list returns to the channel you opened it on. A channel that will not play is a black tile. When the server has no working graphics card for the drawing, the bar has no Multiview button. On Apple TV, Multiview is in the transport bar's menu: tick two to four channels and choose Watch; the arrows move the sound, a click on the touch surface watches the channel with the sound full screen, Menu returns to the list, and Menu from the list to the channel you opened it on.

On an Android TV or Google TV, a pointer remote or a mouse moves a cursor: the control under it takes focus as the D-pad would give it, a click presses it, and the D-pad carries on from there. A cursor left resting where the D-pad scrolls a row under it takes nothing.

On a desktop or in a browser, the arrow keys move like the D-pad, Enter or the keypad's Enter is OK (hold it for a long press), Escape is Back, Page Up and Page Down move a page, the digit keys enter a channel number, C toggles captions and Space or K pauses and resumes. Touch and a mouse select directly. In the full-screen player, a tap, a click or moving the mouse shows the info bar with a Back button at the top left and, under the bar, channel down, pause or play (retry while the stream has failed) and channel up, then the bar's buttons, the volume and full screen; another tap hides it, a tap with the channel list or the stream card open closes it, and a swipe up on a touch screen opens the channel list.

On a Linux desktop the full-screen player appears in the desktop's media controls (MPRIS): the sound menu, the lock screen and phone companions such as KDE Connect show the channel and program, and their play, pause, next and previous buttons pause, resume and change the channel, as the keyboard's media keys do. Windows and macOS do not show it yet.

The players fill the window. On a desktop or in a browser, F in a player, F11 on any page, a double click on the picture or the Full screen button at the end of the player's button row switches the window to full screen and back; a browser also leaves it on its own Escape. On Android the system bars hide while a player is on top, and a swipe from the edge shows them for a moment. On an Android phone or tablet, going home or to another app while a channel, a library item or a recording plays shrinks it into a picture-in-picture window that keeps playing, with play and pause in the window's controls; tap the window to open the app again, or close it to stop the stream. A TV has no picture-in-picture.

On a desktop or in a browser the players have their own volume, which the guide's preview sound follows too. The Mute button and the Volume slider sit at the end of the player's button row, before Full screen; in a full screen player, + (or =) and - step the volume by 5, M mutes and unmutes, and the mouse wheel or a two-finger scroll over the picture steps it up and down. A pill at the top left shows the new level for a moment. The level and the mute stay set across channels and restarts. A TV, a phone and an Apple TV use the system volume and the remote's or the device's volume keys instead.

On a TV the screens keep 48 dp at the sides and 27 dp at the top and bottom clear for overscan. On phones, tablets, desktops and browsers they keep clear of the system bars, camera cutouts and the on-screen keyboard instead. A window narrower than 600 dp shows the guide's channel column as logo and number only, and a window narrower than 600 dp or shorter than 480 dp leaves out the preview so the grid keeps its rows.

A channel opened from Favorites or a category chip keeps channel up and down and the channel list inside that filter, and the info bar names it with the channel's place, for example "Sports 3/214". Typing a number outside it widens them to the whole lineup. Leaving the app stops playback and releases its session on the server. Returning opens a new session for the same channel and prepares the stream at the live edge while preserving paused intent.

The info bar ends with a row of buttons, so a remote with only a D-pad, OK and Back reaches everything the dedicated keys do. Left or Right on the bar moves into the row, where Left and Right move along it, OK presses a button, and Up or Back returns to the picture; Up and Down never change the channel while a button has focus, and the focused button's name shows under the row. The buttons are Channels, which opens the channel list; Previous channel, once you have watched another, which returns to it as a long press of OK does; Pause or Play (Try again while the stream has failed); Captions, which toggles them and hands the remote back to the picture so Left and Right can set the timing; Stream info, which opens the stream card; Zoom (see below); Sleep timer (see below); Watch from start, while the program on now is being recorded, which plays the recording from the program's start; Record, on a server that records, which records the program on now (Cancel recording takes it off again) and says so at the top left; the star, which adds the channel to the favorites or removes it; and Guide, which opens the guide at Now. With a mouse or touch the same buttons follow the channel and pause buttons, with pause and play there instead of in the row.

Zoom on either player's bar, or the remote's Zoom key, steps how the picture meets the screen when their shapes differ: Fit shows all of it with bars at the sides or top, Fill fills the screen and crops what overhangs it (a 4:3 channel loses its side bars), and Stretch stretches the picture to the screen. A note at the top left names the new zoom, and it holds from channel to channel and item to item until the app closes. The guide's preview always fits.

Sleep timer on either player's bar steps, a press at a time, through 15, 30, 60 and 90 minutes, the end of what plays (the program on now, or the library item or recording, whose Up next it skips) and off; the button names what it is set to, as Sleep in 24 min. When it runs out the player leaves for the page it was opened from, as Back would, with nothing playing, so the TV can go to sleep on its own. It lasts through zaps and Up next, and leaving the player turns it off.

The channel list opens on OK while the info bar shows, or from Channels on the bar. Its chips are Recent (the channels watched lately, newest first), Favorites, the guide filter the channel was opened from, and All; Up/Down select a channel, Left/Right move along the chips, OK tunes the focused channel, Back closes the list, and it closes on its own after 8 seconds without a key. Each row shows the channel's number, logo, name, the program on now with its progress, and the next program with its start time. The last channel watched is pinned above the All and filter lists. Picking a channel under All from a filtered player widens channel up and down to the whole lineup.

The info bar shows the channel's logo and the current program's title, badges, episode, time and description, with the program's image when the guide source has one. The first three bars after install end with a one-line key legend, which the stream card carries from then on.

Info held, or Stream info on the bar, opens the stream card: the stream's audio tracks when it offers more than one (focus starts on the one playing; Up/Down and OK pick another, and the choice lasts for the channel), the video codec, picture size, frame rate and bitrate the player reports, the audio codec, on Android how the audio leaves the device (passed through encoded to a receiver or TV, or decoded here to stereo, 5.1 or another layout), the seconds buffered, the server's source for the stream and the session id, and, from a server that reports it, the stream's health as the server sees it: the rate it sends the stream at, how many times the source stalled and how many times the server moved the channel to another source. Back, Info, Left or Right closes it, OK does too when there are no tracks to pick, and it closes on its own after 15 seconds. On a desktop the card lists libVLC's tracks and media information; on iOS it shows the picture size and bitrate only; in a browser it shows the picture size.

Pause shows how many seconds the picture is behind live and that resuming after 30 seconds paused rejoins live; when it does, "Live" shows for a moment. A failed stream's card counts down to the next automatic reconnect, and a channel the server has no working source for says so.

## Links

The app opens `sofaking://` links on Android, Android TV, iOS and Apple TV, so a home automation, a shortcut or another app can start something:

| Link | Opens |
| --- | --- |
| `sofaking://live/NUMBER` | The channel with that number full screen on Live, such as `sofaking://live/5.1`; a channel's name works in place of its number. Back lands on the guide. |
| `sofaking://vod/ID` | The library item with that id in the player, from where the household stopped. Back lands on the Library tab. |
| `sofaking://vod/ID?t=SECONDS` | The same item, that many seconds in. |
| `sofaking://pair?server=URL&code=CODE` | Pairs with that server, as the admin portal's QR code does. With no server saved, or with the one the link names, it pairs at once; with another server saved, a dialog asks "Switch to HOST?" and says that this device stops using the saved one. Cancel is focused first; Switch pairs, Cancel drops the link. The Apple TV app pairs at once. |

On Android and Android TV only `pair` opens from a web page; `live` and `vod` open from another app, from the home screen's Watch Next row, or from `adb shell am start -a android.intent.action.VIEW -d sofaking://live/5.1`, the way Home Assistant's Android TV integration starts an app. A channel or item the server does not have leaves the app where it was. The Apple TV's Top Shelf uses the same `vod` links, and its older `sofaking://channel/ID` links still open the channel.

## Captions

Enable captions with the CC key, the Captions button on the player's bar or in Settings. They show in the guide's preview as well as the player. Recordings show them too, and their player has the same CC key and Captions button; a library item shows only the subtitles picked in its Audio and subtitles card, which CC turns off and on.

Where captions are drawn depends on the platform:

- On Android, the app draws them in the selected caption style.
- On a desktop, libVLC draws them into the picture, and on iOS and Apple TV, AVPlayer draws them in the system's caption style. The app's caption style does not apply. AVPlayer shows only the captions a channel's playlist declares, so the server declares the CEA-608 captions it finds in a stream whose playlist leaves them out.
- In a browser, the app draws them in the selected style while it draws the video into its own canvas. When it falls back to the browser's video element, without WebGL2 or when the server sends no CORS headers on media, the browser draws them. Chrome's built-in HLS player shows none.

On Android, caption timing shifts captions against the picture so live captions align with speech. Press CC, then within three seconds use Right to show them earlier or Left to show them later, in half-second steps, up to 8 seconds either way; each press keeps the window open and the toast shows the timing. The info bar shows the channel's timing as "CC 2.0 s early" or "CC 1.5 s late". Timing is saved per channel; Settings sets the default timing, in the same steps and range. Showing captions earlier buffers the video by that much, so it also means a longer wait when a channel starts; showing them later does not.

## Apple TV

The Apple TV app's Live tab is a guide grid: a row per channel with its number and name, and its programs across 90 minutes under a row of times, from the half hour now is in. Right past a row's last program and Left past its first move the grid half an hour, up to 24 hours ahead; a red mark shows a program set to record. Select a program on now to watch its channel, or a later one for its details. Above the grid the preview plays the focused channel, muted, beside its program's title, times and summary, as on the other apps: its sound comes on after 5 seconds, and after 10 seconds the channel opens full screen, continuing the preview's stream; Settings › Live TV sets that rest (10 or 30 seconds, or never) and turns preview sound off. Each move of focus starts the rest again, muted, the channel just left does not reopen by itself until focus moves, and nothing opens by itself while VoiceOver is on or the preview shows Preview unavailable. All, Favorites (in the order you starred them) and Recent above the grid choose its channels, and Search beside them opens a page that finds a channel by name or number, with what is on it now. Menu from the player returns to the guide on the channel you watched, back at now, showing All when a filter hid it. It opens where you left it, as the other apps do: the last channel full screen, or the Library or DVR tab when you left from there.

On a server that offers it, On later beside the filters shows what is coming up today and tomorrow on those channels in Movies, Sports, Kids and Premieres, soonest first, each with its day, time and channel; select one for its details, with Record, Record series, Add to watchlist and Watch, as on the other apps.

Hold the touch surface on a program for Program details, which shows the program's image, episode, rating, time left, description and what follows, with Watch and the favorite toggle, or add the channel to the favorites from the same menu. On a server that records, the program on now and the one that follows each have Record (Cancel recording once set) and Record series (Stop series once set), and a sports program that names two sides has Record for each team (Stop recording once set), as on the other apps. The program on now also has Add to watchlist (Remove from watchlist once there). More airings lists the program's other airings on any channel: select one on now to watch it, or a later one for its details and Record.

In the player, swipe down for the transport bar: its menu records the program on now on a server that records (Cancel recording once set), stars the channel and lists the recent channels, the favorites and every channel, so switching never leaves the player. Settings › Live TV › Captions shows the closed captions of channels and recordings, and the transport bar's Subtitles panel turns them on and off, which Settings remembers. The panel's On and Off are the Apple TV's own captions setting, so the app's setting turns that on and off with it. While the program on now is being recorded, the menu's Watch from start plays the recording from the program's start, and Menu from it returns to the channel live.

When the server keeps media libraries, a Library tab sits between Live and Settings in the tab bar. It shows Continue watching, the Watchlist, each library's recent additions and the libraries, each a grid of posters with a sort order, an Unwatched filter and a genre menu. Select a card for its page, with Play or Resume, Play from beginning, Mark watched and, for a movie, show or video, Add to watchlist; a show's page lists its seasons and the chosen season's episodes, and selecting an episode plays it, while holding the touch surface on one opens its Details page, starts it over or marks it watched. Play/pause on the remote plays a movie or episode straight from its card, and holding the touch surface on a Continue watching card offers Remove from Continue watching.

The library player is the system player: swipe to scrub anywhere in the item (by its times only: the system player has no place for the server's scrub thumbnails), swipe down for the item's details; in a file the server converts, a jump past what it has converted waits a moment while it converts from there, and while every converter is busy it shows Waiting for a free converter for up to 20 seconds before the error. The app saves the position as it plays. The transport bar's Audio and Subtitles menus pick a track as on the other apps, picture subtitles included, and its Playback Speed menu plays at 1×, 1.25×, 1.5× or 2×. Subtitles there take the look set in the Apple TV's Settings › Accessibility › Subtitles and Captioning.

Inside an episode's intro, Skip intro shows over the picture and jumps to its end; with Settings › Library › Skip intros set to Automatically, it counts down from 5 and skips on its own, and pausing stops it. Inside its end credits, Skip credits shows the same way, or Next episode when the credits run to the end and another episode follows, and Skip credits set to Automatically counts down too. Set to Off, neither shows. At the end of an episode an Up next card offers the next one and plays it after 10 seconds, with Play now and Cancel (or Menu); anything else returns to the page. Select a recording to play it, resuming where you stopped; hold the touch surface on one for From start and Delete (Stop on a running one, Cancel recording on a scheduled one), which asks first. The Settings tab takes the server address and the API token, or a pairing code with Pair; Servers on this network, above them, lists the servers it finds and fills the address in. On a server that shares them, Settings › Channels › Share favorites across devices keeps the favorites, their order and the hidden channels in step with the household's other devices, as on the other apps. ### During ads

On streaming channels that mark their ads, such as Pluto TV, Tubi, Whale, LG, Roku, Samsung TV Plus, some Stirr stations and many on Xumo and Plex, the Sofa King logo bounces around the screen through each break, with the sound off (Logo, muted) or on (Logo, sound on). The screen starts black, and on each bounce takes the color the logo wore into it, with the picture just showing through. In the middle, the screen counts down to the show once the stream says how long the break runs, from the start on cued breaks such as Tubi's and near the end on stitched ones such as Pluto TV's. Under it are a line picked for the break, the program you're watching, a fact about it that changes every 8 seconds (the episode, what TMDB says about the show or movie when the server has a TMDB token, such as when it first aired, its seasons, cast and creators, and what is on next), and the ads dodged today, which Settings → Live TV → During ads shows too; a corner hit gets a line of its own. The time sits in the top corner, and the logo squashes against each wall it hits, unless the device turns animations off (Remove animations on Android, Reduce Motion on Apple TV). OK or Back shows the picture until that break ends; zapping and the info bar work as ever. On a desktop the screen can come and go a few seconds off the break's edges, since libVLC does not say how much it has loaded.

### Update check

Opening Settings asks GitHub once per app start for the newest build of the running build's channel: a snapshot compares its build number with the rolling `snapshot` prerelease, and a release, such as 0.2.0, compares its version with the latest release. The line reads Sofa King fresh when it is up to date, Update available with the published version, or Couldn't reach GitHub; Check again asks once more. A local build reports the catalog version, so it compares with the latest release. When an update is available, What's new lists the upgrade notes and new features and then the fixes since the running build (of every snapshot since it, or of the newer release), up to six lines and a count of the rest, from the release notes.

With an update available on Android, Install update downloads the package inside the app, checks it against the SHA-256 GitHub records for it, and checks that it is Sofa King, signed with the same key as the running app and of a higher version code, before handing it to Android's installer. The first time, Android asks to allow Sofa King to install apps and to confirm the install; on Android 12 and later, later updates install without a prompt once you choose Install update. Sofa King closes while the update installs; open it again afterward. A build an app store installed (Google Play, F-Droid or Amazon) offers no Install update, and a package signed with another key is refused before Android sees it, as [troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md#why-will-a-snapshot-apk-not-install-over-my-build) explains. On a desktop or in a browser, Get the update opens the release page; a Linux desktop with the apt source from [getting started](getting-started.md#desktop) takes each snapshot with its other system updates instead. No package is published for iOS or tvOS, and the line says so. Nothing downloads or installs until you choose to.

### Settings lock and kids mode

With a PIN set, opening Settings shows a lock page instead: enter the PIN and select Unlock. The PIN keeps a child or a guest out of Settings, the channel manager and the startup picker; watching, zapping, the CC key, favorites in the player and pairing from a `sofaking://pair` link are not affected. The unlock lasts until you leave Settings. After five wrong PINs in a row, Unlock waits 30 seconds. The PIN is stored on this device as a salted hash, never on the server, and does not sync to other devices.

Forgot the PIN, on the lock page, takes the server's API token instead: the admin portal shows it under Settings, Advanced, Reveal the API token, and a match removes the PIN. On a server that runs without a token, the PIN can only be removed by clearing the app's data on the device.

With a PIN set, Turn on kids mode, under Parental controls, locks Settings again and returns to the guide in kids mode, and the header shows Kids next to the app's name on a TV. The guide, the channel list and zapping keep only the channels filed under a kids' group (one whose name has Kids or Children in it), and a channel outside them, from a link or a number, does not play. The library keeps only titles rated G, PG, TV-Y, TV-Y7, TV-G or TV-PG on its shelves, its grids and in search; a title without a rating is hidden, and a library's row of letters goes away. The DVR tab is hidden, and on Android TV the home screen's Watch Next row is emptied. Kids mode filters channels by their group, not programs by their rating, since guide ratings vary by source. To leave it, open Settings, enter the PIN and select Turn off kids mode. Kids mode is this device's own and does not sync. On Apple TV the channel list's title reads Sofa King Kids, and the Top Shelf shows only kids mode's channels and titles.

### Channel manager

Manage channels, under Live TV, lists the favorites in the order the guide's Favorites filter and the player's channel list show them; the arrows beside a favorite move it. Channels not yet placed follow by number. Below, the lineup's groups (the guide's chips) each have a switch and a count of how many of their channels are shown: switched off, every channel of the group leaves the guide and the channel list on this device only, so the server and other devices keep it; switched on, they all return. The arrow beside a group opens its channels as switches of their own, so one channel can be hidden or shown by itself; a channel filed under two groups appears under both and follows the last switch that touched it. Skip hidden channels with Channel Up and Down takes them out of zapping too; a hidden channel entered by number still plays, with the full lineup around it.

On a server that keeps a shared copy, Share favorites across devices sits at the top of Manage channels, off by default. Turned on, the device keeps its favorites, their order and its hidden channels the same as every other device of the household that has it on: it takes the household's list over when another device saved one, otherwise it saves its own as the household's, and each change after that reaches the others the next time they start. The recent list stays each device's own.
