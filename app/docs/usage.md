# Using Sofa King

This page covers the guide, the library, recordings, the channels the guide lists, the remote and keyboard controls, captions and the settings. [Getting started](getting-started.md) connects the app to a server first.

## Guide

The app opens where you left it. Left from Live, it opens full screen on the channel you last watched; Back shows the guide focused on that channel. Left from Library or Recordings, it opens that tab, with nothing playing. When you have not watched a channel yet, or that channel is no longer in the lineup, it opens the guide. Settings → Open on start can open the guide, the library or one chosen channel instead. With no server set, the guide shows Connect to your Sofa King server with the header's Settings button focused; press OK and [connect to the server](getting-started.md#3-connect-to-the-server).

The Now chip, the Guide key and the Menu key return to the current time on the focused channel. Fast Forward and Rewind move the grid a whole day ahead and back, up to six days, keeping the time of day and the focused channel; the range above the channel names reads Tomorrow or the weekday, and a day's programs load the first time you move to it. Search filters by channel name, number or current program, and lists a channel's later programs that match on a Later today line under its row, each with its start time; OK on one moves the grid to that program. The Search key starts voice search through the TV's speech recognizer and focuses the first match; the Search chip, a TV without a recognizer, or a dismissed recognizer open the text field instead. All, Favorites, Recent (the last ten channels watched full screen for at least 10 seconds, newest first) and category chips select channel groups, and the app reopens on the chip you chose last. Movies now, Sports now, Kids now and News now list the channels whose current program the guide files under that kind; each shows only while some channel has one, and the lists are read again every five minutes.

The focused channel plays in a muted preview after a 350 ms delay. After focus has rested on it for 5 seconds the preview's sound comes on, and after 10 seconds the channel opens full screen, continuing the preview's stream. On Android the muted preview leaves other apps' sound playing and takes the sound over only when its own comes on, and a channel that opened by itself lets the TV's screen saver start until you press a key. Settings → Live TV sets that delay (Off, 10 or 30 seconds) and turns preview sound off. The channel never opens by itself while TalkBack touch exploration on Android or VoiceOver on iOS is on, or while the preview shows Preview unavailable. Any key press restarts both timers and mutes the preview again. A touch or mouse press or scroll restarts them too, but after one the channel does not open by itself until the next key press, so scrolling the guide by hand never opens a channel under your finger. OK opens that channel full-screen at once. Back returns to the guide focused on the channel you were watching, including one you changed to in the player, with the preview muted. A filter or search that hides that channel switches back to All, and a time cursor left in the past moves to now. The channel just left does not reopen by itself until focus moves to another channel. The Info key, or holding OK on a program, opens its details: the image, title, episode, time with how long is left or until it starts, rating and description, with Watch, Add to favorites (or Remove from favorites) and Close; on a TV the details open in a panel beside the grid, elsewhere in a sheet. On a server that records, a program that has not ended also has Record, which schedules it (Cancel recording takes it off again), and Record series, which records every airing of its title on that channel (Stop series deletes the rule and the recordings it scheduled, keeping the ones already made); the server's [recordings](../../backend/docs/recordings.md) page describes what happens next. When the server does not take the change, a line under the buttons says so. In the guide, Back moves from the grid to the selected filter chip, and from the chips to the open tab in the header; from there Back exits the app. Open search closes first.

Each channel shows its logo beside its number. The details above the grid show the focused program's episode label and title, a New badge for a first airing, its rating and its image, when the guide source has them. Times follow the device's 12- or 24-hour clock setting. A channel without programs says why: No listing when its guide source lists nothing then, No guide for this channel when the server has no guide source mapped to it, and Guide out of date when the guide source's data has run out.

The app shows its saved lineup while refreshing. Channels load before guide data. Automatic refresh runs every three hours and when the app returns to the front; Settings → Server → Reload requests a new load.

## Library

When the server keeps media libraries, the app shows a Library tab beside Live. On a TV both tabs sit in the header next to the app's name, Up from the guide's chips reaches them, and Settings stays the header's last button; on a phone the tabs are a bar along the bottom, and on a tablet, desktop or browser a rail at the side, with Settings as the last item; the bar or rail stays on every page but the full-screen players. On a TV the Guide key (or Menu) on any Library or Recordings page opens the guide at Now. The Library tab reads its shelves again each time it is opened. A server without libraries shows no Library tab; the app asks again every five minutes, so the tab appears within five minutes of the first library being added.

The Library tab opens with a Search chip and a chip for each library at the top, then Continue watching (what you stopped partway through and the next episode of a show you are watching) and each library's recent additions. Search finds movies, shows, episodes and videos by title, show or cast across every library as you type; the remote's Search key on the Library tab starts it by voice, and Back returns to the Library tab. A card without a poster shows a frame from the video, or the library mark when there is none. A library opens as a grid of posters with chips to sort by title, recently added, year, rating or recently watched and to show only what is unwatched, and a row of the library's genres to show only one; more load as you scroll. Cards show a bar for how far in you are, a tick once watched and, on a show, how many episodes are unwatched. OK opens an item's page; the remote's Play key plays a movie or episode straight from its card, and holding OK on a card (a long touch or a right click elsewhere) opens a menu with Play from beginning for something partway in and Mark watched or Mark unwatched. An item page's overview that runs past four lines takes focus, and OK shows the rest. Back goes from an item's page to where you opened it and from a library's grid to the Library tab; on the Library tab it moves focus up to the tab in the header, and from there opens Live with focus on its tab, where one more Back exits.

An item's page shows its backdrop, title, year, length, rating, genres and badges for the picture (4K, HD, HDR), a line with the sound of its first audio track, its audio and subtitle languages and the studio or network, the overview, who directed it, and its cast as a row of chips; OK on a cast member searches the libraries for that name, and Back returns to the page. Focus starts on Play, or Resume from the time you stopped, with Play from beginning beside it and Mark watched or Mark unwatched after; an episode's or a season's page ends the row with Go to show, which opens the show's page. A show's page plays or resumes its next episode, lists its seasons in a row of chips (opening on the season with that episode, which takes focus when you come down to the row) and the season's episodes under them, each with its own still; OK on an episode plays it, resuming where you stopped.

Playing a library item opens the full-screen player. Left and Rewind step back 10 seconds and Right and Fast Forward 30; holding the key moves a minute and then five at a time. The target shows on the bar under the title and plays once you stop pressing, or at once on OK; Back while moving cancels it, and Back otherwise returns to the item's page with the episode you watched focused. OK, Play/Pause, Play and Pause pause and resume, and Up, Down or Info show the bar, which shows where you are, when the item ends at the current time if it plays on, and its length. When the file has more than one audio track or has subtitles, Down on the bar opens Audio and subtitles (a button beside the controls with a mouse or touch): picking another audio track plays the item again from where it is, and subtitles are drawn over the picture. A forced subtitle track, which carries only the dialogue the audio does not, is on from the start; the others start off. The tracks you pick in a show are kept on the device, by language, and its other episodes start with them. Subtitles inside the file and subtitle files beside it, such as the ones Bazarr downloads, are listed, the files marked External; when a show starts its next episode with your subtitle language, a file in that language wins over the subtitles inside the video. Picture subtitles (PGS from Blu-ray, VobSub from DVD) are drawn into the picture by the server, so picking one, or turning one off, plays the item again from where it is. The server plays a file your device can play as it is; for anything else it converts the file as it plays, and a jump past what it has converted restarts it there, which takes a moment. Where you stop is saved every 10 seconds, when you leave and when another app comes to the front; the server marks an item watched past 90%. Returning to the app picks up where you were. When an episode ends, an Up next card shows the next one, the next in its season or the first of the next season (Specials aside), and plays it after 10 seconds; OK or Play plays it at once and Back returns to the page. Anything else, or a show's last episode, returns to the page at the end.

## Recordings

On a server that records, the app shows a Recordings tab beside Live and Library. It lists what is recording now, then what was recorded, newest first, then what is scheduled, soonest first, with the recordings folder's free space at the top. Each row shows the title, the episode, the channel and when it aired, and whether it was watched, failed or is still recording. Play opens a recording in the full-screen player; one started before shows Resume, which picks up where the household stopped, and From start. A recording still being made plays as it grows. Commercial breaks the server found show on the seek bar, and Settings → Recordings → Skip commercials makes playback jump over them. The first recording takes focus when the tab opens. Stop, Cancel or Delete ends a running recording, takes a scheduled one off or deletes a finished one and its file, after a second press confirms it. Stopping within the last tenth of a recording marks it watched. Below the recordings, Series lists the series rules: Record repeats too (or New episodes only) changes which airings a rule takes, Keep steps how many recordings it keeps through All, 1, 3, 5 and 10, and Stop series deletes the rule and the recordings it scheduled after a second press, keeping the ones already made. A rule's channel, padding and priority are set on the server's portal. Back goes from a recording to the Recordings tab, from the tab's list up to the tab in the header, and from there to Live with focus on its tab, where one more Back exits.

## Channels

Channels come from the server's lineup. The app lists a channel when the server reports it as playable and gives it a numeric display number, and lists them in number order. The server tags each channel with its lineup group, such as News, and each tag is a guide category with its own chip; a channel with several tags is under each, and one without a tag is under Other. Chips show how many channels they list.

## Remote controls

| Key | Guide | Player |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Long-press OK | Open the program's details | Return to the previously watched channel |
| Channel Up / Down | Move a page (4 rows) | The next higher / lower channel number |
| Captions (CC) | | Toggle captions |
| Play/Pause | | Pause or resume; resume after 30 seconds returns to live |
| Digits | Move to the row with that number, showing All when the filter hides it; OK or a 1.5-second pause commits | Enter a channel number; OK or a 1.5-second pause tunes it |
| Next / Previous | | The next higher / lower channel number, as Assistant's next and previous do |
| Last channel | | Return to the previously watched channel, also after picking a channel in the guide |
| Info | Open the program's details | Toggle the info bar; held, open the stream card |
| Fast Forward / Rewind | A day ahead / back | |
| Guide / Menu | Jump to Now (on a Library or Recordings page, open the guide at Now) | Open the guide at Now |
| Search | Search by voice | Open guide search by voice |
| Back | Close search; move from the grid to the selected filter chip, then to the header's open tab; exit from the header | Close the channel list or the stream card, or return to the guide |

On a desktop or in a browser, the arrow keys move like the D-pad, Enter or the keypad's Enter is OK (hold it for a long press), Escape is Back, Page Up and Page Down move a page, the digit keys enter a channel number and C toggles captions. Touch and a mouse select directly. In the full-screen player, a tap or click shows the info bar with a row of icon buttons under it for the guide, channel down, pause or play (retry while the stream has failed), channel up, the channel list, captions and the stream card; another tap hides it, a tap with the channel list or the stream card open closes it, and a swipe up on a touch screen opens the channel list.

On a TV the screens keep 48 dp at the sides and 27 dp at the top and bottom clear for overscan. On phones, tablets, desktops and browsers they keep clear of the system bars, camera cutouts and the on-screen keyboard instead. A window narrower than 600 dp shows the guide's channel column as logo and number only, and a window narrower than 600 dp or shorter than 480 dp leaves out the preview so the grid keeps its rows.

A channel opened from Favorites or a category chip keeps channel up and down and the channel list inside that filter, and the info bar names it with the channel's place, for example "Sports 3/214". Typing a number outside it widens them to the whole lineup. Leaving the app stops playback and releases its session on the server. Returning opens a new session for the same channel and prepares the stream at the live edge while preserving paused intent.

The channel list opens on OK, Left or Right while the info bar shows. Its chips are Recent (the channels watched lately, newest first), Favorites, the guide filter the channel was opened from, and All; Up/Down select a channel, Left/Right move along the top row, OK tunes the focused channel, Back closes the list, and it closes on its own after 8 seconds without a key. Each row shows the channel's number, logo, name, the program on now with its progress, and the next program with its start time. The last channel watched is pinned above the All and filter lists. Picking a channel under All from a filtered player widens channel up and down to the whole lineup.

The end of the top row holds the current channel's buttons, so a remote with only a D-pad, OK and Back reaches everything the dedicated keys do: the star adds the channel to the favorites or removes it, Captions toggles them and closes the list so Left and Right can set the timing, Pause or Play (Retry while the stream has failed) does what the Play/Pause key does, and Stream info opens the stream card.

The info bar shows the channel's logo and the current program's title, badges, episode, time and description, with the program's image when the guide source has one. The first three bars after install end with a one-line key legend, which the stream card carries from then on.

Info held, or Stream info in the channel list, opens the stream card: the stream's audio tracks when it offers more than one (focus starts on the one playing; Up/Down and OK pick another, and the choice lasts for the channel), the video codec, picture size, frame rate and bitrate the player reports, the audio codec, the seconds buffered, the server's source for the stream and the session id. Back, Info, Left or Right closes it, OK does too when there are no tracks to pick, and it closes on its own after 15 seconds. On a desktop the card lists libVLC's tracks and media information; on iOS it shows the picture size and bitrate only; in a browser it shows the picture size.

Pause shows how many seconds the picture is behind live and that resuming after 30 seconds paused rejoins live; when it does, "Live" shows for a moment. A failed stream's card counts down to the next automatic reconnect, and a channel the server has no working source for says so.

## Captions

Enable captions with the CC key or in Settings. They show in the guide's preview as well as the player.

Where captions are drawn depends on the platform:

- On Android, the app draws them in the selected caption style.
- On a desktop, libVLC draws them into the picture, and on iOS, AVPlayer draws them in the iOS caption style. The app's caption style does not apply.
- In a browser, the app draws them in the selected style while it draws the video into its own canvas. When it falls back to the browser's video element, without WebGL2 or when the server sends no CORS headers on media, the browser draws them. Chrome's built-in HLS player shows none.

On Android, caption timing shifts captions against the picture so live captions align with speech. Press CC, then within three seconds use Right to show them earlier or Left to show them later, in half-second steps, up to 8 seconds either way; each press keeps the window open and the toast shows the timing. The info bar shows the channel's timing as "CC 2.0 s early" or "CC 1.5 s late". Timing is saved per channel; Settings sets the default timing, in the same steps and range. Showing captions earlier buffers the video by that much, so it also means a longer wait when a channel starts; showing them later does not.

## Apple TV

The Apple TV app lists the channels, favorites first, with the program on now and next; select one to watch it. It opens where you left it, as the other apps do: the last channel full screen, or the Library or Recordings tab when you left from there. Hold the touch surface on a channel for Program details, which shows the program's image, episode, rating, time left, description and what follows, with Watch and the favorite toggle, or add the channel to the favorites from the same menu. On a server that records, the program on now and the one that follows each have Record (Cancel recording once set) and Record series (Stop series once set), as on the other apps. In the player, swipe down for the transport bar: its menu stars the channel and lists the recent channels, the favorites and every channel, so switching never leaves the player. When the server keeps media libraries, a Library tab sits between Live and Settings in the tab bar. It shows Continue watching, each library's recent additions and the libraries, each a grid of posters with a sort order, an Unwatched filter and a genre menu. Select a card for its page, with Play or Resume, Play from beginning and Mark watched; a show's page lists its seasons and the chosen season's episodes, and selecting an episode plays it, while holding the touch surface on one starts it over or marks it watched. Play/pause on the remote plays a movie or episode straight from its card. The library player is the system player: swipe to scrub anywhere in the item, swipe down for the item's details; in a file the server converts, a jump past what it has converted waits a moment while it converts from there. The app saves the position as it plays. The transport bar's Audio and Subtitles menus pick a track as on the other apps, picture subtitles included. At the end of an episode an Up next card offers the next one and plays it after 10 seconds, with Play now and Cancel (or Menu); anything else returns to the page. On a server that records, a Recordings tab follows Library: it lists what is recording now, what was recorded, newest first, and what is scheduled, with Skip commercials at the top. Select a recording to play it, resuming where you stopped; hold the touch surface on one for From start and Delete (Stop on a running one, Cancel recording on a scheduled one), which asks first. The commercial breaks the server found show on the player's bar, and with Skip commercials on, playback jumps over a break it runs into. The Series section below lists the series rules, with Record repeats too or New episodes only, Keep, and Stop series, which asks first. The Settings tab takes the server address and the API token, or a pairing code with Pair. The Home screen's Top Shelf shows the last ten channels watched, newest first, while the app is in the top row; selecting one opens the app on that channel.

## Settings

Settings opens from the header's Settings button on a TV and from the navigation bar or rail elsewhere. On a TV focus starts on the first setting and the remote's Back key is the way back, so Settings and its pages draw no Back button until a mouse or touch is used; elsewhere focus starts on Back. Back returns to the tab it was opened from, with focus on the Settings button. Switches and choices apply at once. The sections run General, Live TV, Recordings (on a server that records), Captions, Playback (Android), Server, Lock and About. The Server section reads Connected to with the server's address, a Change button and Reload, which loads the lineup and the library again; Change opens the server fields, which apply when you select Save & reload under them, and Cancel closes them. On a device with no server yet, Server is the first section, with its fields open and focus on Server URL.

| Section | Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

A chosen or last channel the lineup no longer has opens the guide, and a tab the server no longer offers opens Live |

| Live TV | Open focused channel automatically | After 10 seconds | How long focus rests on a channel before it opens full screen: Off, after 10 or after 30 seconds. TalkBack touch exploration on Android and VoiceOver on iOS turn it off |
| Live TV | Play preview sound | On | Whether the preview's sound comes on after 5 seconds |
| Live TV | Guide rows | Comfortable | The height of the guide's rows; Compact fits more channels on screen |
| Live TV | Manage channels | | Opens the channel manager, below |
| Recordings | Skip commercials | Off | On a server that records. Playback of a recording jumps over the commercial breaks the server found |
| Captions | Closed captions | Off | Shows captions in the player and the preview. The CC key toggles it too |
| Captions | Smooth live captions | On | Android only. Holds roll-up text until each line is complete, so captions appear a line at a time |
| Captions | Caption style | System | Where the app draws captions, as [captions](#captions) lists. System follows Android's caption preferences, or white on black in a browser; Large, high contrast overrides them |
| Captions | Default caption timing | As broadcast | Android only. How far captions are shown earlier or later on channels without their own timing |

About shows the version and how many channels are loaded, then whether a newer build is published, and the notice for the TMDB details the server can add to libraries; the Apple TV app shows that notice under About too. On a snapshot, every build shows a version ending in `-dev.RUN+COMMIT`, the workflow run number and the short commit; only the desktop packages' own version, which the installer shows, is the plain one.

### Update check

Opening Settings asks GitHub once per app start for the rolling `snapshot` prerelease and compares its run number with the running build's: the line reads Up to date, Update available with the published version, or Could not reach GitHub; Check again asks once more. A build without a snapshot suffix cannot be compared, and the line shows the published version instead. With an update available, Get the update opens the Android package's download address on Android (a phone or tablet installs it through the browser; an Android TV usually has nothing to open it with, so the address is shown and the package is installed with `adb install -r`), or the release page on a desktop or in a browser. No package is published for iOS or tvOS, and the line says so. The app never downloads or installs anything by itself.

### Settings lock

With a PIN set, opening Settings shows a lock page instead: enter the PIN and select Unlock. The PIN keeps a child or a guest out of Settings, the channel manager and the startup picker; watching, zapping, the CC key, favorites in the player and pairing from a `sofaking://pair` link are not affected. The unlock lasts until you leave Settings. After five wrong PINs in a row, Unlock waits 30 seconds. The PIN is stored on this device as a salted hash, never on the server, and does not sync to other devices.

Forgot the PIN, on the lock page, takes the server's API token instead: the admin portal shows it under Settings, Advanced, Reveal the API token, and a match removes the PIN. On a server that runs without a token, the PIN can only be removed by clearing the app's data on the device.

### Channel manager

Manage channels, under Live TV, lists the favorites in the order the guide's Favorites filter and the player's channel list show them; the arrows beside a favorite move it. Channels not yet placed follow by number. Below, the lineup's groups (the guide's chips) each have a switch and a count of how many of their channels are shown: switched off, every channel of the group leaves the guide and the channel list on this device only, so the server and other devices keep it; switched on, they all return. The arrow beside a group opens its channels as switches of their own, so one channel can be hidden or shown by itself; a channel filed under two groups appears under both and follows the last switch that touched it. Skip hidden channels with Channel Up and Down takes them out of zapping too; a hidden channel entered by number still plays, with the full lineup around it.
